Contractual SLA, Incorporation Rights & Delay Attribution Rules

1. Contractual Client Review SLA (10 Working Days)

The contractual benchmark for Client engineering review turnaround is strictly 10 Working Days (Monday–Friday, excluding weekends) from receipt of transmittal (Actual Submit In) to return of transmittal (Actual Submit Out). If review ≤ 10 WD, Client passed SLA. If review > 10 WD, Client exceeded SLA.

2. Contractor Planned Incorporation Allowance & Entitlement Rights

In the Contractor's Rebaseline Plan, the planned duration ($\Delta T_{\text{plan}}$) between consecutive revisions implicitly accounts for Client's 10 WD review. Therefore, the Contractor's entitled incorporation allowance is:

Contractor Planned Allowance = max(0, Plan Cycle WD - 10 WD)

Crucial Contractual Rule: If Client takes longer than 10 WD (e.g. 15 WD), the Contractor maintains the right to their full planned incorporation duration. Thus, the Allowable Extended Cycle before Contractor is considered in delay is:

Allowable Extended Cycle = Actual Client Review WD + Contractor Planned Allowance

If Contractor submits within this allowable cycle, the delay is 100% attributable to Late Client Review, not Contractor!

3. Delay Root Cause Attribution Decision Tree

  • (1) Improperly Planned (Baseline Scheduling Flaw): Plan Cycle ≤ 10 WD. The Contractor scheduled Revision N+1 within ≤ 10 working days of Revision N. Since the Client is contractually entitled to 10 WD for review, this left ≤ 0 working days for Contractor engineers to incorporate comments. The plan was doomed to fail before execution started.
  • (2) Late Contractor Incorp (Discipline Execution Flaw): Client completed review within contractual SLA (≤ 10 WD), but the Contractor's engineering discipline took longer than their planned incorporation allowance to re-submit.
  • (3) Late Client Review (Client SLA Overrun): Actual Cycle > Plan Cycle, BUT Actual Cycle ≤ Allowable Extended Cycle (Client took > 10 WD; Contractor used their entitled time). The entire delay is solely attributable to Client review.
  • (4) Compound Delay (Shared Overrun): Both Client review took > 10 WD AND Contractor took > planned incorporation allowance (exact days quantified per party).

Note: (1) and (2) are separated because (1) reflects a structural planning failure by the Contractor's planning team (allocating zero incorporation time), whereas (2) reflects an execution bottleneck by the engineering disciplines (exceeding a valid incorporation window). Both are Contractor responsibilities.

4. Macro Evaluation Justification Rule

If the percentage of delayed transitions driven by Contractor incorporation overruns (Late Contractor + Compound Delay) exceeds 50%, it confirms that the Contractor's Rebaseline Plan was systematically over-optimistic across the board.
